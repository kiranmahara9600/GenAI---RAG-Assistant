# Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs)

A collection of repeatable step-by-step guides for common workplace tasks.

---

## How to Onboard a New Employee

Step 1 — Two weeks before start date
Send the offer letter and employment contract. Confirm start date, time, and location. Assign an onboarding buddy from the same team.

Step 2 — One week before start date
Submit an IT request to provision laptop, email, Slack, GitHub, and Jira access. Order any requested peripherals. Add the new hire to the team calendar and relevant Slack channels.

Step 3 — Day before start date
Send a welcome email with: building address, arrival instructions, parking info, who to ask for at reception, and the onboarding buddy's contact details.

Step 4 — Day one morning
Greet the new hire at reception. Hand over laptop and access badge. Sit with IT while setup completes (allow 1–2 hours). Schedule a welcome lunch with the team at noon.

Step 5 — Day one afternoon
Walk through the employee handbook together. Complete mandatory security and compliance training enrollment. Do a tour of the office: kitchen, meeting rooms, IT helpdesk.

Step 6 — End of week one
Check that all system access is working. Confirm the new hire has scheduled 1:1s with each team member. Collect feedback on the onboarding experience via the HR portal survey.

Step 7 — Week two to week twelve (probation)
Hold check-in meetings at weeks 2, 6, and 12. Document progress and feedback in the HR portal. At week 12, complete the probation review form and submit to HR within 5 business days.

## How to Submit and Approve an Expense

Step 1 — Collect your receipt
Keep the original receipt for every business expense. Digital receipts (email or PDF) are accepted. Handwritten receipts are not accepted.

Step 2 — Check the expense policy
Expenses under $50 do not require prior approval. Expenses over $50 require written manager approval before being submitted. Business travel must always be booked through the company travel portal first.

Step 3 — Log in to the expense portal
Go to intranet > Finance > Expense Portal. Click "New Expense Claim."

Step 4 — Fill in the claim form
Enter: date, amount, currency, category (travel / meals / software / other), business justification, and project code if applicable. Attach the receipt scan or photo.

Step 5 — Submit the claim
Click Submit. You will receive a confirmation email. Claims submitted by Thursday are typically processed in the following week's payroll cycle.

Step 6 — Manager approval (expenses over $50)
Your manager receives an approval request by email. They must approve or reject within 5 business days. If rejected, you will receive a reason and can resubmit with amendments.

Step 7 — Reimbursement
Approved expenses are reimbursed via payroll on the next pay date. Check your payslip to confirm receipt. Contact finance@company.com if reimbursement has not appeared within two pay cycles.

## How to Request and Set Up Software Access

Step 1 — Check if a licence already exists
Before requesting new software, search the IT portal (intranet > Tools > Software Catalogue) to see if the company already has a licence available for your role.

Step 2 — Submit a software request ticket
Go to IT portal > New Ticket > Software Request. Fill in: software name, version, business justification, number of seats needed, and your manager's name for approval routing.

Step 3 — Manager approval
Your manager will receive an email to approve or deny the request. Approvals typically take 1–2 business days. IT will not proceed without manager sign-off.

Step 4 — IT procurement and provisioning
Once approved, IT procures the licence (allow 3–5 business days for new software). For existing licenced tools, access is usually granted within 24 hours.

Step 5 — Installation
You will receive an email from IT with either: a download link, an invite to the SaaS platform, or instructions to visit the IT helpdesk for device installation. Follow the instructions in that email.

Step 6 — Confirm access
Test that the software works with your credentials. If you encounter issues within the first 48 hours, reply to the IT email or raise a follow-up ticket referencing your original request number.

Step 7 — Annual licence review
All software licences are reviewed annually in Q4. IT will email you to confirm whether you still require access. Non-responses result in licence removal to reduce costs.

## How to Run a Team Retrospective Meeting

Step 1 — Schedule the meeting
Book a 60-minute slot within one week of a sprint or project milestone. Invite the full team. Use a neutral facilitator (ideally not the team lead) to encourage open discussion.

Step 2 — Prepare the retrospective board
Set up a shared board (Notion, Miro, or a whiteboard) with three columns: What went well / What didn't go well / What we'll do differently.

Step 3 — Silent brainstorm (10 minutes)
Each participant writes their points individually on sticky notes or digital cards — one idea per card. No discussion yet. This prevents groupthink and ensures quieter team members contribute equally.

Step 4 — Share and group (15 minutes)
Each person reads out their cards and places them on the board. The facilitator groups similar items into themes without editing content.

Step 5 — Discuss and prioritise (20 minutes)
The team votes (dot voting: 3 votes each) on the most important items to discuss. Work through the top-voted items. Focus on patterns, not blame.

Step 6 — Define action items (10 minutes)
For each item to improve, agree on a specific action, an owner, and a deadline. Action items must be SMART: Specific, Measurable, Assignable, Realistic, Time-bound.

Step 7 — Document and follow up
The facilitator writes up the action items in the team's Notion page within 24 hours. At the next retrospective, start by reviewing whether previous action items were completed.
